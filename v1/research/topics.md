# Paper Airplanes — research topics

Round 1 list. Plain words, with pictures in `research/index.html` once built. Copies of other people's pages go in `research/private/` (not committed).

| # | Topic | Why it matters for the sheets | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The four forces**: lift, weight, drag, thrust (the throw) | The one-page "how a plane flies" picture | Written (research page) |
| 2 | **Lift from wings**: air pushed down, wing tilted up (angle of attack) | Why wide-wing gliders float and darts don't | Written |
| 3 | **Balance point (centre of gravity)** vs. where the lift pushes | Why most planes put the heavy folded paper at the nose; a "pinch test" to find the balance point | Written |
| 4 | **Trim**: bending the back edges up (elevators) or one side (rudder) | The fix list on each test card: dives → bend up, stalls → bend down, turns → bend the other way | Written |
| 5 | **Dihedral**: wings tilted up into a slight V | Steadier flight; a step on most sheets | To write |
| 6 | **Folding precision**: sharp creases, matching corners, symmetry | Small errors make a plane turn; why we crease with a fingernail or ruler | To write |
| 7 | **Fold diagrams**: valley (dashed), mountain (dash-dot), arrows | The standard from origami diagrams (Akira Yoshizawa's notation, 1950s, later Randlett and Harbin), so Felix can read any book later | Used on every sheet |
| 8 | **Paper**: weight (20 lb vs 24 lb), grain | Heavier paper flies farther but is harder to fold; test both | To test |
| 9 | **Printing at 100%**: printer margins and two-sided alignment | Alignment test sheet built (⚙️) | To test |
| 10 | **History**: early paper gliders, Leonardo's flying-machine sketches, the Wright brothers' glider tests (1900–1902) | Written |
| 11 | **Simulators** | How a simple glide simulator works (a few numbers per plane); only if wanted later | Far later |
